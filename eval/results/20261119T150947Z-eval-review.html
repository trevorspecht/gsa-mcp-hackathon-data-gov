Most useful starting points

I found and inspected three HUD catalog records. CHAS looks strongest for local housing-needs analysis; HADS is more useful for historical household-level affordability research. These are metadata-based assessments—not findings from downloaded data.

1. Comprehensive Housing Affordability Strategy (CHAS)

Usefulness: A good starting point for studying housing problems and needs among low-income households, including planning for housing assistance. HUD describes it as custom Census tabulations not generally available in standard Census products.

Still needs checking:

  • Which observation periods and geographic levels are currently available.
  • Exact affordability and housing-problem definitions, denominators, and renter/owner breakdowns.
  • Download options and actual API functionality—the catalog labels the distribution “API,” but that has not been tested.
  • Access and reuse terms, which are not clarified in this record.

Source: HUD CHAS query/data page
Dataset identifier: HUD044

2. ACS 5YR CHAS Estimate Data by County

Usefulness: The most concrete candidate for county comparisons and mapping. Its description explicitly identifies 2016–2020 ACS coverage and household counts organized around housing problems and income thresholds, primarily 30%, 50%, and 80% of HUD Area Median Family Income. The record lists CSV, Excel, geospatial downloads, and an ArcGIS REST service.

Still needs checking:

  • Actual fields, county identifiers, coverage gaps, and join compatibility.
  • Which measures isolate affordability versus other housing problems.
  • Documentation on uncertainty, suppression, and appropriate comparisons.
  • Whether a newer release is available. The metadata’s 2025 modification date does not make these 2025 observations.

Source: HUD county CHAS dataset
ArcGIS item identifier: dbcc9ba4a4144a6d823b4d429d2b97c6

3. Housing Affordability Data System (HADS)

Usefulness: Promising for historical analysis of housing-unit affordability and owner/renter housing-cost burdens. It derives from the American Housing Survey and classifies households and units relative to income, Fair Market Rent, and poverty benchmarks.

Important limitation: The inspected description says 2013 is the latest public-use file; 2015 onward is available only as an internal-use file, with access inquiries directed to HUD.

Still needs checking: Current access arrangements, available survey years and metro coverage, variable documentation, survey weights, and comparability across releases.

Source: HUD HADS page
Dataset identifier: HUD031

Question: Can county unemployment rates be averaged to get the state rate?

Not with a simple average. The state unemployment rate is the share of the state’s labor force that is unemployed—not the average of county percentages.

Use data from the same period with consistent definitions and adjustment status. For an exact published state figure, use the official state series: rounding and differences in estimation or seasonal adjustment can keep a county-based calculation from matching it exactly.
