Several useful government datasets cover drought conditions, irrigated land, and irrigation water use in the western United States.

Dataset Coverage and period What it provides
Western Land Data Assimilation System—WLDAS — NASA Western U.S. and surrounding region; daily, 0.01° grid; catalog period Jan. 2, 1979–Jan. 1, 2024 Modeled soil moisture, snow depth and snow-water equivalent, evapotranspiration, groundwater recharge, and anomalies. Useful for historical drought and water-resource assessment—not direct measurements of irrigation withdrawals.
Vulnerability of runoff to the 2015 western U.S. drought — USGS Western U.S. river basins; focused on the 2015 drought Measures assessing streamflow vulnerability to drought. A targeted research dataset rather than an ongoing drought-monitoring series.
LANID: Landsat-based irrigation dataset for CONUS 2018–20 — USGS Conterminous U.S., including western states; annual irrigation maps for 2018–2020 Irrigated-land mapping. The record also describes a monthly 2000–2020 irrigation-water-use reanalysis by HUC12 subwatershed, including evapotranspiration, irrigated area, consumptive use, and effective precipitation. The title and description refer to different scopes; confirm the files available on the release page.
Landsat-derived Rainfed and Irrigated-Cropland Product, L3, Version 2 — USGS / NASA LP DAAC Currently conterminous U.S.; 30-m resolution; nominal 2020, derived from 2019–2021 imagery Maps irrigated versus rainfed cropland, non-cropland, and water bodies. Useful for locating irrigation, not measuring water volume. Despite “Global” in its title, the inspected description says current coverage is CONUS.
